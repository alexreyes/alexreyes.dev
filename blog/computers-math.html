Computers and math



So, if you've done lots of algebra or any sort of math, eventually you start to get tired of the rote activity of practicing a concept once you've gotten it. Yes, you think, “I understand how to add. Why do I have to keep adding things for the rest of my life whenever I use math? Why can’t we move on from addition once we reach algebra, or something?”.



Imagine you lived in a time before computers, and you had to use math in your work. At some point, you'd probably start dreaming of a calculating machine, one that could take out the tedium of your work and leave you to do the more fun parts of your job.


Because every day, even though you’re working on cool new ideas in architecture, you must first calculate whether what you’re envisioning is even possible. And that requires, well, lots of math to figure out. But, even though the math you get to use is interesting to you, you start to get tired of having to do basic arithmetic in order to get to the solution all the time. You start to feel like a robot. So that’s what leads you to dreaming of a robot that will do the calculations for you.


You think “If I had this calculating machine, I could spend more time thinking about how to design buildings, and what techniques I can use to model them, instead of doing all this rote arithmetic! Not to mention, even though you have a pretty good grasp of math, you do occasionally make small arithmetic mistakes which end up derailing an entire calculation. And when these mistakes happen multiple layers into a calculation, well, that can be pretty frustrating! So your machine would also have the additional specification that it wouldn’t make mistakes with arithmetic. This could work because it would just be applying the same process over and over without ever letting its mind wander. Wow, what a great machine this could be!


As you think about this arithmetic calculation machine, you realize that since math builds on itself, perhaps this machine could be made to be capable of so much more than just doing arithmetic for you. You realize that if the machine could do arithmetic, it would have much of the necessary capabilities to get expanded to the most basic forms of algebra, all the way up to calculus and the most complex forms of math that we have. Wow, an arithmetic calculator machine could do so much!


So as you dream about this computer, you dream about what other things we could do with a powerful calculating machine beyond just solving your math problems.


You realize that the purpose of math, since ancient times, has been to solve problems in the real world, by breaking them down into models that we can understand and easily manipulate. These models of the world are very battle tested, and proven to work. So, they're pretty valuable!


And that's when you start to wonder: what can't be represented with math?


So you toy around a little bit, thinking of ways to represent different things with math, and realize there's tons of things that can be represented with math. From physics to art, all the way to understanding animal populations, and more.


And you realize, that although the models we use for these subjects get close to the reality, making them true to reality usually requires extremely complicated calculations (because reality is messy). Wow, you think “this arithmetic machine could really do a lot! It may eventually be better than we are at modeling reality! This could be really cool. I bet it could be better at testing my architectural ideas than me!”


And you continue on, figuring out how to represent art with math, and so on and so forth. After a bit of this, you realize that wow, this computing machine may very well be pretty powerful, because it allows us to work with one of the most fundamental ways we have of representing the world: math.


Anyways, as all these ideas start to hit you, your boss walks in and asks you to do another set of calculations. Well, you think, maybe you can build this machine on another day.
